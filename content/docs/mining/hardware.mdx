## Overview

Kaspa's kHeavyHash proof-of-work algorithm can be mined with both GPUs and purpose-built ASICs. This page focuses on durable hardware-selection and operations guidance rather than model-by-model market claims.

<Callout type="warn">
Hardware specs, profitability, and model rankings change frequently. Treat this page as operational guidance, not a live benchmark sheet. Before purchasing hardware, verify current performance and power data from vendor datasheets and up-to-date community/pool telemetry.
</Callout>

## GPU Mining

GPU mining was the original method for mining Kaspa and remains accessible to hobbyist miners. Both AMD and NVIDIA GPUs can mine kHeavyHash effectively.

### NVIDIA GPUs

NVIDIA cards are widely used for kHeavyHash testing and hobby mining. Actual results depend heavily on model, silicon quality, driver version, and tuning profile.

### AMD GPUs

AMD cards are also commonly used and can perform well with appropriate tuning. On either vendor stack, efficiency and stability matter more than headline hashrate alone.

<Callout type="info">
Hashrate and power can vary significantly with drivers, miner version, clocks, voltage, ambient temperature, and silicon quality.
</Callout>

### GPU Overclocking Tips

To maximize kHeavyHash performance on GPUs:

- **Core clock matters most**: Unlike memory-heavy algorithms (such as Ethash), kHeavyHash is more sensitive to core clock speed than memory speed. Prioritize core overclocks.
- **Reduce memory clock**: You can often lower memory clocks to save power with minimal hashrate impact.
- **Power limit tuning**: Start at the default power limit and reduce gradually. Some cards keep most of their hashrate even with meaningful power-limit reductions.
- **Thermal management**: Follow vendor thermal guidance, use conservative fan curves, and ensure adequate airflow.

```bash
# Example NVIDIA overclock using nvidia-smi (Linux)
nvidia-smi -i 0 -pl 250          # Set power limit to 250W
nvidia-smi -i 0 -lgc 1500        # Lock core clock to 1500 MHz
```

## ASIC Mining

Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) designed for kHeavyHash are specialized devices optimized for this workload. In many deployments they offer better efficiency than general-purpose GPUs, but economics depend on current network conditions and electricity price.

### ASIC Comparison Table

Use current vendor specifications when comparing ASICs. Focus on:

- Rated hashrate and power at the same operating mode.
- Efficiency at the wall (not only chip-level specs).
- Cooling/noise profile for your deployment environment.
- Reliability, warranty terms, and firmware support cadence.
- Delivery timelines and importer/shipping constraints.

<Callout type="warn">
ASIC pricing and availability fluctuate significantly based on KAS price and network difficulty. Always calculate profitability at current difficulty and KAS price before purchasing. A machine that is profitable today may not be profitable if difficulty rises or KAS price falls.
</Callout>

### ASIC Setup Basics

Most Kaspa ASICs ship with a web-based management interface:

1. **Connect the miner** to your local network via Ethernet.
2. **Find the miner's IP** using your router's DHCP client list or a network scanner.
3. **Access the web UI** by navigating to the miner's IP in a browser.
4. **Configure pool settings**: Enter your pool's stratum URL, your Kaspa wallet address, and a worker name.
5. **Monitor**: The web UI displays hashrate, temperature, fan speed, and pool connection status.

```
Pool URL:    stratum+tcp://<your-pool-host>:<port>
Worker:      kaspa:qr...your_wallet_address.worker01
Password:    x
```

## Market Dynamics

Mining hardware economics change over time as network hashrate, device efficiency, and KAS price move. In practice:

- **Efficiency matters**: More efficient hardware generally has a larger margin under rising difficulty.
- **Difficulty is shared**: As global hashrate rises, expected rewards per unit hashrate decline for all miners.
- **GPU viability is situational**: Existing hardware, low electricity costs, and non-profit goals can make GPU mining reasonable for some operators.

<Callout type="info">
kHeavyHash remains GPU-mineable, but profitability depends on current economics. GPU miners should carefully calculate electricity costs versus expected rewards before committing.
</Callout>

## Choosing Hardware

### Decision Factors

| Factor | GPU | ASIC |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Usually lower per unit | Usually higher per unit |
| Resale value | High (GPUs have general-purpose uses) | Low (only useful for kHeavyHash) |
| Efficiency | Lower in most deployments | Higher in most deployments |
| Noise | Moderate (manageable at home) | High (rack units require dedicated space) |
| Flexibility | Can switch to other algorithms | Locked to kHeavyHash |
| Setup complexity | Moderate (driver tuning, OC) | Simple (plug, configure pool, run) |
| Profitability at scale | Usually lower in ASIC-dominated eras | Usually higher when economics are favorable |

### Recommendations

- **Hobbyist/experimenter**: Start with hardware you already own, or with a small low-power unit, and validate thermals/noise before scaling.
- **Serious miner**: Compare current-generation ASICs using live economics (difficulty, KAS price, uptime assumptions, and electricity rate).
- **GPU farm operator**: Benchmark kHeavyHash against your alternative workloads and switch based on current economics.

## Power and Cooling Considerations

Mining hardware generates significant heat and draws substantial power:

- **Electrical capacity**: Ensure your electrical infrastructure can handle continuous high loads; rack ASICs can require substantial dedicated capacity.
- **Cooling**: High-performance ASICs are often loud and produce concentrated heat. Plan for adequate ventilation or dedicated mining rooms. Immersion cooling is an option for some large-scale operations.
- **Electricity cost**: This is typically the largest ongoing expense. Calculate your cost per kWh and compare against expected mining revenue under conservative assumptions.

```
Daily revenue = (your_hashrate / network_hashrate) * daily_block_rewards * KAS_price
Daily cost = power_consumption_kW * 24 * electricity_rate_per_kWh
Daily profit = Daily revenue - Daily cost
```

<Callout type="warn">
Always run profitability calculations with conservative estimates. Network hashrate tends to increase over time, and cryptocurrency prices are volatile. What is profitable today may not be profitable in a month.
</Callout>
