## Overview

Every block in the Kaspa DAG includes a **coinbase transaction**. Under current consensus, coinbase outputs are built from mergeset reward data, while the current block's subsidy is encoded in the coinbase payload. Emission follows the **chromatic phase**, which smoothly reduces subsidy over time rather than using abrupt halvings.

## Current Block Reward

The block reward decreases monthly according to the chromatic emission formula. To find the current reward, you can query any Kaspa block explorer or calculate it from the current DAA score using the formula described below.

At 10 blocks per second, the network produces approximately **864,000 blocks per day**. The total daily emission equals the per-block reward multiplied by the number of blocks produced.

## Chromatic Emission Schedule

Kaspa uses a **chromatic emission** model inspired by the musical chromatic scale. Instead of halving the reward at fixed intervals (as Bitcoin does every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years), Kaspa reduces the reward by a factor of `(1/2)^(1/12)` every month. This means:

- The reward halves every **12 months** (since `((1/2)^(1/12))^12 = 1/2`).
- Each monthly reduction is approximately **5.6%**.
- The reduction is smooth and predictable — there is no single moment where miners suddenly lose half their revenue.

<Callout type="info">
The name "chromatic" comes from the fact that there are 12 reductions per halving, analogous to the 12 semitones in a musical chromatic scale. Each "note" represents one month of emission.
</Callout>

## Reward Calculation from DAA Score

The block reward is computed as a function of the block's **DAA score** (a consensus-time counter used for subsidy and lock-time logic). The calculation works as follows:

1. Determine which **chromatic phase** the current DAA score falls into. Each phase corresponds to one month of block production.
2. Look up the **base reward** for that phase from the emission table.
3. Coinbase construction routes rewards according to mergeset blue/red rules.

The emission table is hardcoded in the consensus rules. Every full node independently verifies that each block's coinbase pays the correct reward for its DAA score.

## Blue Blocks vs Red Blocks

In GHOSTDAG consensus, blocks are classified as **blue** (honest, well-connected) or **red** (late, potentially adversarial). This classification affects rewards:

- **Blue blocks**: When a block is blue, its subsidy and eligible fees are paid via later merging coinbase construction to that blue block's reported script.
- **Red blocks**: Red-block rewards are redirected through merging coinbase construction to the miner of the merging blue block.

This mechanism ensures that miners are incentivized to build well-connected blocks that arrive on time. Miners who produce blocks that end up red (due to high latency, withholding, or other reasons) forfeit their reward.

<Callout type="warn">
If your blocks are frequently marked red, you are losing rewards. Common causes include high network latency, an out-of-sync node, or mining on a stale tip. Ensure your node is well-connected and fully synced.
</Callout>

## Coinbase Maturity

Coinbase outputs (newly minted KAS from block rewards) cannot be spent immediately. They require a maturity period of **1,000 DAA score** before they become spendable (at 10 BPS). This prevents miners from spending rewards from blocks that might later be reorged out of the selected parent chain.

At 10 BPS, 1,000 DAA score corresponds to roughly **100 seconds** of network time, making coinbase maturity very short compared to Bitcoin's 100-block (~16.7 hour) maturity period.

## Reward Per Second

At 10 BPS, the total emission rate in KAS per second is:

```
emission_per_second = block_reward * 10
```

This figure is useful for estimating mining revenue. If the current block reward is `R` KAS, and your hashrate represents a fraction `f` of the total network hashrate, your expected revenue per second is:

```
revenue_per_second = R * 10 * f
```

## Revenue Estimation for Miners

To estimate your mining revenue:

1. **Determine your hashrate share**: Divide your hashrate by the total network hashrate.
2. **Calculate expected blocks per day**: Multiply your hashrate share by 864,000 (blocks per day).
3. **Multiply by the current block reward**: This gives your expected daily KAS earnings.

Keep in mind that this is a statistical average. Actual results will vary, especially for solo miners. Pool miners will see more consistent payouts that converge on this estimate over time.

## Comparison with Bitcoin's Halving Model

| Property | Kaspa | Bitcoin |
|---|---|---|
| Reduction frequency | Monthly (~5.6% each) | Every ~4 years (50% each) |
| Reduction factor | `(1/2)^(1/12)` per month | `1/2` per halving |
| Halving period | ~12 months (smooth) | ~210,000 blocks (~4 years, abrupt) |
| Miner revenue shock | Minimal (gradual decline) | Significant (sudden 50% drop) |
| Time to 50% supply mined | About ~1 year from schedule start under current constants | ~4 years |

The chromatic model is designed to avoid the economic disruption that Bitcoin halvings cause, where miners suddenly lose half their revenue overnight.

## Total Supply Progression

Under the current consensus schedule, total emission converges to approximately **28.38 billion KAS** (about 28.376B, with a small high-BPS rounding delta). Because the reward decreases by `(1/2)^(1/12)` each month, emission follows a converging geometric-style schedule with a long diminishing tail.

<Callout>
Emission and supply are enforced by consensus rules for the active protocol version. Changing them requires a consensus upgrade (hard fork).
</Callout>
