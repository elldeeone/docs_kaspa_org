## Current State

Following the activation of the [Crescendo hardfork](/docs/protocol-evolution/crescendo), Kaspa operates at **10 blocks per second** using the GHOSTDAG consensus protocol with K=124. The network produces approximately 864,000 blocks per day and a faster confirmation cadence than the pre-Crescendo 1-BPS regime.

For current protocol constants, see [Network Parameters](/docs/reference/network-params) and KIP-14 / `rusty-kaspa` consensus configuration.

Kaspa's reference node implementation, `rusty-kaspa`, is written in Rust and actively maintained by a global community of contributors.

<Callout type="info">
Kaspa does not have a centralized development team with a fixed roadmap timeline. The items described on this page reflect the current direction of research and development as discussed publicly by contributors. Priorities, timelines, and specific approaches are subject to change based on research outcomes and community consensus.
</Callout>

## Near-Term Goals

### DAGKnight Consensus

`DAGKnight` appears in the KIPs repository as [KIP-2](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0002.md) with status `Proposed`.
Any consensus transition would require acceptance through the KIP process and hardfork activation.

<Callout>
For deployment details, defer to the accepted KIP text and `rusty-kaspa` implementation at the time of activation.
</Callout>

### Further Block Rate Increases

The currently active baseline is 10 BPS from KIP-14. Additional block-rate changes, if proposed and accepted, would require new KIP status progression and hardfork activation.

## Medium-Term Goals

### Smart Contract Layer

Kaspa's scripting capabilities are currently limited to basic transaction scripts. The addition of transaction introspection opcodes (KIP-10) and the development of more expressive scripting are steps toward a broader smart contract capability:

- **Transaction introspection (KIP-10).** Adding opcodes that allow scripts to inspect transaction properties, enabling covenants and conditional spending logic.
- **Transient mass (KIP-13).** Refining the mass model to more accurately price computational resources, which is a prerequisite for efficient smart contract execution.
- **Contract execution environment.** Research into what form a smart contract layer should take — whether it is a native scripting extension, an embedded VM, or a layered architecture.

Specific external prototypes and application-layer experiments are outside the canonical scope of `rusty-kaspa` and the KIPs index. For protocol decisions, treat accepted/active KIPs and merged `rusty-kaspa` changes as authoritative.

A recurring design tradeoff in KIP discussions is balancing programmability with auditability and consensus safety.

### Archival Node Improvements

As the network operates at 10 BPS and potentially higher in the future, the volume of data grows proportionally. Potential archival-node improvement areas include:

- More efficient storage formats for historical block data.
- Better indexing for historical transaction queries.
- Optimized initial block download (IBD) for new archival nodes joining the network.

Pruned nodes already keep storage requirements bounded, but archival nodes (which retain the full history) require ongoing optimization to remain practical.

## Long-Term Vision

### Proof-of-Work Hardware Trajectories

Consensus does not mandate any specific mining hardware trajectory. Hardware-performance and energy-efficiency outcomes are external to protocol rules and should be treated as implementation/economics questions rather than consensus guarantees.

### Maximum Decentralization

The long-term vision for Kaspa is a network where:

- Block rates are high enough for real-time applications.
- Node operation remains accessible on consumer hardware.
- Mining is distributed across a large, geographically diverse set of participants.
- Protocol governance remains open and permissionless.

Every protocol change is evaluated against these decentralization goals. Throughput increases that appear to significantly raise centralization pressure would generally receive additional scrutiny.

<Callout type="warn">
This roadmap describes research directions and community aspirations, not commitments. Kaspa has no corporate entity making binding promises about timelines. Each item requires a KIP, community consensus, rigorous testing, and a successful hardfork activation. Some items may be deprioritized, redesigned, or abandoned based on what the research and engineering work reveals.
</Callout>

## Research Areas

Active roadmap themes that are directly reflected in current KIPs/repository state include:

- **DAGKnight track ([KIP-2](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0002.md), Proposed).** Potential future consensus evolution beyond fixed-`k` operation.
- **Mass-model evolution ([KIP-9](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0009.md) / [KIP-13](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0013.md), Active).** Ongoing refinement of transaction/resource pricing.
- **Script and transaction capability expansion ([KIP-10](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0010.md), Active).** Incremental programmability improvements at the script layer.
- **Operational scaling at current and future BPS.** Parameter and implementation work required for stable higher-throughput operation.

## Community Governance

All protocol changes follow the [KIP process](/docs/protocol-evolution/kips-overview). This means:

- Anyone can propose a change.
- Proposals undergo public technical review.
- Activation requires broad consensus among node operators, miners, and the wider community.
- In practice, major protocol changes have required broad operator and ecosystem coordination.

This governance model is slower and less predictable than a centrally managed roadmap, but it ensures that Kaspa's evolution reflects the genuine consensus of its participants rather than the priorities of a single organization.
