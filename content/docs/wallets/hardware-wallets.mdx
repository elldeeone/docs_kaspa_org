## Why Use a Hardware Wallet?

A hardware wallet is a dedicated physical device that stores your private keys in a secure element, isolated from your computer and the internet. When you sign a transaction, the signing happens entirely on the device — your private keys never leave the hardware. This protects you against:

- **Malware and keyloggers** on your computer.
- **Clipboard hijacking** that could alter destination addresses.
- **Phishing attacks** that trick you into entering your seed phrase on a fake website.
- **Remote access attacks** where an attacker gains control of your machine.

<Callout>
A hardware wallet is the recommended way to store significant amounts of KAS. Even if your computer is compromised, an attacker cannot extract your private keys from the hardware device.
</Callout>

For day-to-day spending, a [Web Wallet](/docs/wallets/web-wallet) or KasVault is convenient. For long-term storage or large holdings, a hardware wallet provides a substantially stronger security model.

## Ledger

### Overview

Kaspa is supported on **Ledger** hardware wallets via the official Kaspa app. The Kaspa Ledger app is available on both the **Ledger Nano S Plus** and **Ledger Nano X** devices. It allows you to store KAS, sign transactions, and manage addresses with the private keys secured inside the Ledger's certified secure element (CC EAL5+).

### Installing the Kaspa App

1. Connect your Ledger device and unlock it with your PIN.
2. Open **Ledger Live** on your computer.
3. Navigate to **My Ledger** (the app catalog).
4. Search for **Kaspa** in the app catalog.
5. Click **Install** to load the Kaspa app onto your device.

<Callout type="info">
Ensure your Ledger device is running the latest firmware before installing the Kaspa app. You can check for firmware updates in Ledger Live under **My Ledger > Device Settings**.
</Callout>

### Using Ledger with a Companion App

The Ledger device itself does not have a standalone interface for managing your Kaspa balance. You need a **companion application** that communicates with the Ledger to display your balance, construct transactions, and request signatures from the device.

Currently supported companion apps for Kaspa with Ledger include:

- **Kaspa Web Wallet** ([wallet.kaspa.org](https://wallet.kaspa.org)) — The official Kaspa web wallet supports connecting to a Ledger device via WebUSB. When connected, the wallet uses the Ledger to sign transactions rather than a software key.
- **KasVault** — The KasVault browser extension can also interface with Ledger devices for transaction signing.

#### Connecting Your Ledger

1. Plug in your Ledger device and unlock it.
2. Open the **Kaspa app** on the Ledger (navigate to it on the device screen and press both buttons).
3. Open your companion application (e.g., the Web Wallet).
4. Select the option to connect a hardware wallet.
5. Your browser will prompt you to select the Ledger device via WebUSB — select it and confirm.
6. The companion app will derive your addresses from the Ledger and display your balance.

#### Signing a Transaction

When you initiate a send transaction in the companion app:

1. The companion app constructs the unsigned transaction.
2. The transaction details are sent to the Ledger device.
3. The Ledger displays the **recipient address** and **amount** on its screen.
4. **Verify the address and amount on the Ledger's physical screen** — this is the critical security step, as the Ledger's screen cannot be manipulated by malware.
5. Press both buttons on the Ledger to approve the transaction.
6. The Ledger signs the transaction internally and returns the signature to the companion app.
7. The companion app broadcasts the signed transaction to the network.

<Callout>
Always verify the recipient address and amount on the Ledger's physical screen before approving. The Ledger's display is the only trustworthy source of transaction details — your computer screen could be showing tampered information if malware is present.
</Callout>

### Ledger Address Format

Addresses derived by the Ledger follow the same Schnorr-based scheme used by all Kaspa wallets. The derivation path is compatible with the standard Kaspa BIP-32 path (`m/44'/111111'/0'/0/index`), so addresses generated by the Ledger will match those generated by a software wallet using the same seed phrase.

### Recovery

Your Ledger device is protected by a 24-word recovery phrase that is generated when you first initialize the device. This recovery phrase is **not specific to Kaspa** — it covers all apps on your Ledger. If your device is lost or damaged, you can restore your Kaspa wallet (and all other wallets) on a new Ledger device using this recovery phrase.

<Callout type="info">
The Ledger recovery phrase and your Kaspa wallet seed phrase are the same 24 words if the Ledger was used to create the wallet. If you imported an existing Kaspa seed phrase into a Ledger, the Ledger's own recovery phrase is a separate set of words that protects the device itself.
</Callout>

## Tangem

### Overview

**Tangem** cards are NFC-based hardware wallets in a credit-card form factor. They provide private key storage without batteries or cables — you tap the card against your NFC-enabled smartphone to sign transactions.

Kaspa is supported on Tangem cards through the **Tangem app** (available on iOS and Android). Tangem provides a simple user experience for users who want hardware-level security without managing a traditional hardware wallet device.

### Setup

1. Download the **Tangem app** from the App Store or Google Play.
2. Open the app and tap your Tangem card against your phone's NFC reader.
3. Follow the setup instructions to initialize the card and create a Kaspa wallet.
4. The private key is generated directly on the Tangem card's secure chip and never leaves it.

### Sending and Receiving

- **Receiving**: The Tangem app displays your Kaspa address and QR code. Share this with the sender.
- **Sending**: Enter the recipient address and amount in the Tangem app. When prompted, tap your Tangem card to sign the transaction via NFC.

### Backup and Recovery

Tangem uses a multi-card backup system rather than a seed phrase. During setup, you can link up to three Tangem cards together. If one card is lost, the others can still access your funds. This approach eliminates the risk of seed phrase exposure but means your backup is physical (the backup cards themselves).

<Callout>
Tangem's backup model is fundamentally different from seed-phrase-based wallets. You cannot recover a Tangem wallet using a 24-word mnemonic. Store your backup cards as securely as you would store a seed phrase written on paper.
</Callout>

## Choosing a Hardware Wallet

| Feature | Ledger Nano S Plus / Nano X | Tangem |
|---|---|---|
| Form factor | USB device with screen | NFC card (credit card size) |
| Connection | USB (WebUSB) / Bluetooth (Nano X) | NFC (smartphone) |
| Seed phrase backup | 24-word mnemonic | Multi-card backup (no mnemonic) |
| Display | On-device OLED screen | Phone screen (via Tangem app) |
| Multi-coin support | Extensive (5,000+ assets) | Extensive (via Tangem app) |
| Open source | App code is open source | Firmware is audited, partially open |
| Price range | Mid-range | Low to mid-range |

Both options provide strong key isolation. The Ledger is the better choice if you want on-device transaction verification (the physical screen) and seed-phrase-based recovery. Tangem is the better choice if you prefer a simpler, phone-based experience and do not want to manage a seed phrase.

## Security Best Practices

- **Buy directly from the manufacturer.** Do not purchase hardware wallets from third-party resellers or secondary markets. Tampered devices can be pre-loaded with known seed phrases.
- **Verify the device is genuine.** Both Ledger and Tangem provide authenticity checks in their companion apps.
- **Keep firmware updated.** Install firmware updates promptly to receive security patches.
- **Store your recovery phrase / backup cards securely.** Use a fireproof safe, bank safety deposit box, or another secure location.
- **Test with a small amount first.** Before transferring large holdings, send a small test transaction to verify everything is working correctly.
