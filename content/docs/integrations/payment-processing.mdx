## Overview

Kaspa's 10 blocks per second throughput and low transaction fees make it well suited for payment processing. This guide covers how to accept KAS payments in your application, from generating invoice addresses to confirming payments and handling edge cases.

## Payment Flow

A typical KAS payment flow follows these steps:

1. **Generate a unique address** for the payment (one address per invoice).
2. **Display the address** to the customer (QR code and/or text).
3. **Monitor the address** for incoming transactions.
4. **Track confirmation depth** until the payment is considered settled.
5. **Mark the invoice as paid** and fulfill the order.

## Generating Payment Addresses

Each invoice should use a unique Kaspa address. This allows you to unambiguously associate incoming funds with a specific order or customer. Use HD derivation to generate addresses from a master seed:

```
m/44'/111111'/0'/0/0  → Invoice #1
m/44'/111111'/0'/0/1  → Invoice #2
m/44'/111111'/0'/0/2  → Invoice #3
...
```

Store the mapping between invoice IDs and address indices in your database. See [HD Derivation](/docs/wallets/hd-derivation) for implementation details.

<Callout type="info">
Never reuse addresses across invoices. Address reuse makes it impossible to determine which invoice a payment corresponds to and reduces privacy for both you and your customers.
</Callout>

## Monitoring for Payments

### UTXO Subscription

The most efficient way to detect payments is by subscribing to UTXO change notifications from a Kaspa node:

1. Connect to your Kaspa node via wRPC.
2. Call `notifyUtxosChanged` with the list of active invoice addresses.
3. When a payment arrives, the node sends a notification with the new UTXO details (address, amount, transaction ID).

This approach is event-driven and does not require polling.

### Polling Alternative

If event-driven notifications are not feasible in your architecture, you can poll `getUtxosByAddresses` at regular intervals. Given Kaspa's 10 BPS block rate, polling every 1-2 seconds provides near-real-time detection.

## Confirmation Tracking

After detecting a payment, track its confirmation depth before considering it settled. The confirmation depth is measured in **blue score** — the number of blue blocks added to the selected parent chain since the transaction was included.

### Recommended Confirmation Depths

| Use case | Blue score depth | Approximate time |
|---|---|---|
| Digital goods, low value | 10-50 | 1-5 seconds |
| Retail point-of-sale | 100 | ~10 seconds |
| E-commerce | 500 | ~50 seconds |
| High-value goods | 1,000-10,000 | ~2-17 minutes |

<Callout>
Kaspa's 10 BPS rate means that even conservative confirmation requirements resolve in seconds to minutes. For most retail transactions, a confirmation depth of 100 (roughly 10 seconds) provides strong security.
</Callout>

## Webhook/Callback Architecture

A production payment processor typically uses an asynchronous architecture:

1. **Payment service**: Runs a persistent connection to the Kaspa node, subscribing to UTXO changes for all active invoice addresses.
2. **Event processing**: When a payment notification arrives, the service records the payment in a database and begins tracking confirmation depth.
3. **Webhook delivery**: Once the payment reaches the required confirmation depth, the service fires a webhook to your application backend with the payment details (invoice ID, amount, transaction ID).
4. **Idempotency**: Ensure webhook handlers are idempotent — the same webhook may be delivered more than once.

## Handling Underpayments and Overpayments

Real-world payments do not always match the invoice amount exactly:

### Underpayments

If a customer sends less than the invoiced amount:

- **Option A**: Reject the payment and show the remaining balance due, allowing the customer to send a second transaction to cover the difference.
- **Option B**: Accept partial payment and adjust the order accordingly.

Since Kaspa addresses can receive multiple UTXOs, you can sum all UTXOs on an invoice address to determine the total received.

### Overpayments

If a customer sends more than the invoiced amount:

- Record the overpayment and issue a refund for the excess.
- Refunds are simple: construct a transaction sending the difference back to the customer's address.

<Callout type="warn">
Always confirm the customer's refund address explicitly. Do not assume the sender's address is the correct refund destination — in a UTXO system, the "sender" address is actually the change output, which may not belong to the customer.
</Callout>

## Refund Processing

To issue a refund:

1. Obtain the customer's refund address (do not infer it from the payment transaction).
2. Construct a transaction from your hot wallet to the refund address.
3. Sign and broadcast the transaction.
4. Provide the transaction ID to the customer as proof of refund.

## Point-of-Sale Considerations

Kaspa's fast block rate makes it practical for in-person point-of-sale (POS) payments:

- **Display a QR code** with the payment address and amount. Use the `kaspa:` URI scheme for wallet compatibility.
- **Real-time feedback**: With 10 BPS, the first confirmation arrives within 1 second. Display a progress indicator showing confirmation depth.
- **Settlement in ~10 seconds**: A confirmation depth of 100 blue score (approximately 10 seconds) is sufficient for most retail transactions.
- **No chargebacks**: Kaspa transactions are irreversible once confirmed. This eliminates chargeback fraud that merchants face with card payments.

## Integration with E-Commerce Platforms

To integrate KAS payments with an e-commerce platform:

1. **Build a payment plugin** that generates a unique address for each checkout session.
2. **Run a payment service** that monitors addresses and fires callbacks to the e-commerce backend.
3. **Handle order status updates**: Mark orders as "payment pending" when the address is displayed, "payment detected" when the first UTXO appears, and "payment confirmed" when sufficient depth is reached.
4. **Currency conversion**: If pricing in fiat, use a rate API to calculate the KAS equivalent at checkout time and display it to the customer.
